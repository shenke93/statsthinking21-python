Data: qualitative and quantitative

Measurements: discrete and continuous
- Construct: not a physical feature but instead an unobservable theoretical concept.
- Impossible to measure a construct without some amount of error.
- Gold standard: a standard against which other measurements can be tested.
- **Realiable**: test-retest reliability (how well the measurement agree if the same measurement is performed twice). An unreliable measure can never have a strong statistical relationship with any other measure.
- **Valid**: make sure that we are actually measuring the construct that we think we are measuring.
  - Face validity.
  - Construct validity: is the measurement related to other measurements in an appropriate way?         
    - Convergent validity: the measurement should be closely related to other measures that are thought to reflect the same construct. 
    - Divergent validity: measurements thought to reflect different constructs should be unrelated.
  - Predictive validity: truly valid measurements should be predictive of other outcomes.
- Scale: identity (unique meaning), magnitude, equal intervals, absolute zero
  - Nominal
  - Ordinal
  - Interval
  - Ratio
